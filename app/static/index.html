Mars Rover API

An open, unofficial replacement for the archived Mars Rover Photos API.

This is an independent community project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or operated by NASA or JPL. Photos and metadata come from the public raw image feeds and archives published by NASA/JPL.

Migrating

Moving from the old Mars Rover Photos API takes three steps:

  1. Change the host and keep the rest of the URL. 
    https://api.nasa.gov/mars-photos/...  →  $origin/mars-photos/...
  2. No key needed. A key from api.nasa.gov can stay in your requests: it is ignored, so existing clients keep working unchanged. For a higher rate limit, get a key of ours (see API keys).
  3. Both paths work: $origin/api/v1/... and $origin/mars-photos/api/v1/... serve the same API.

API keys

A key is optional. Without one, the rate limit is counted per client IP: 60 requests per minute, 2000 per hour, 4 at a time. With a key it is counted per account, and all keys of one account share it: 120 per minute, 10,000 per hour, 8 at a time. The key works on v1 and v2.

How to get a key

  1. Open $origin/keys in the web interface, or send POST /api/v2/keys/requests with {"email": "user@example.org"} (Content-Type: application/json).
  2. We email you a one-time link. It works for 60 minutes after the email is sent.
  3. Open the link and press the button. The key is shown once on that page: copy it right away. It looks like si_ followed by 32 letters and digits.
curl -X POST "$origin/api/v2/keys/requests" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"email": "user@example.org"}'
# 202 {"status": "accepted", "mail_delayed": false}

The answer is the same for every valid address, so it does not tell whether an address is known. Up to 3 emails per address in 24 hours are sent; more requests get the same answer but no email. "mail_delayed": true means the server has trouble sending email right now and will retry, so the email may come late. If it does not come, check your spam folder, then ask again later. An account can have 2 active keys.

How to send the key

Use the X-Api-Key header. It is preferred: it stays out of proxy logs and browser history. The api_key query parameter works too. If both are present, the header is used.

curl "$origin/api/v2/account" -H "X-Api-Key: si_EXAMPLEEXAMPLEEXAMPLEEXAMPLE0000"

GET /api/v2/account shows your keys, the number of requests per day and your limits. An api_key value that is not in our format (an api.nasa.gov key, DEMO_KEY) is ignored and the request is served without a key. An X-Api-Key header that is not in our format gives 401 invalid_api_key on v2.

A bad or revoked key does not fall back to the no-key limit: the request fails. On v1 the same problems answer 403 in the old error format ({"errors": "Invalid API key"}, {"errors": "API key revoked"}). Many wrong keys from one IP in a short time lead to 429 for keys from that IP. Bodies of key requests must be JSON up to 2 KiB (415 and 413 otherwise).

What we store about you

For a key we store your email address, the dates when the account and keys were created and confirmed, your keys (only as a hash, so we cannot show them again) and the number of requests per day. We need the email to issue the key and to reach you in case of abuse. We send you only the emails you asked for, and we do not give your address to anyone. Emails are sent through $mail_provider. Unconfirmed requests are deleted within a day, request counters after 90 days, and an account with no requests for 24 months is deleted automatically.

You can delete your data at any time: on $origin/keys/manage with your key (DELETE /api/v2/account), or, if you lost the key, on $origin/keys/delete with a link sent to your address. You can also reply to any email from us.

Examples

v1, compatible with the old API:

curl "$origin/mars-photos/api/v1/rovers/curiosity/photos?sol=1000&camera=fhaz"
curl "$origin/mars-photos/api/v1/rovers/perseverance/latest_photos"
curl "$origin/mars-photos/api/v1/manifests/opportunity"

v2, the new API:

curl "$origin/api/v2/rovers"
curl "$origin/api/v2/rovers/perseverance/photos?sol=100&per_page=50"
curl "$origin/api/v2/photos?rovers=curiosity,perseverance&date_min=2026-11-06&sort=-taken_at&per_page=50"
curl "$origin/api/v2/photos/stats?rovers=perseverance&group_by=month"
curl "$origin/api/v2/photos/batch?ids=81234,81235,900000000"
curl "$origin/api/v2/rovers/curiosity/radiation?sol_min=1600&sol_max=1700"
curl "$origin/api/v2/rovers/perseverance/weather?sol_min=100&sol_max=110"
curl "$origin/api/v2/rovers/perseverance/weather/100"

Stats counts photos and sols with the first and last date, grouped by rover, camera, year or month (group_by). sample_type accepts only full; date_min/date_max filter by the date of the sol, not of each photo. Batch takes 1 to 100 comma-separated ids; ids that are not found are returned in missing instead of a 404.

GET /api/v2/photos searches several rovers at once: rovers (comma-separated slugs; default all), sol/sol_min/sol_max or earth_date/date_min/date_max (not both), camera (up to 10 codes), sort (-sol, sol, -taken_at, taken_at), local_time_min/local_time_max or golden_hour=true, min_width/min_height, and near=LAT,LON with radius_km (one rover only: curiosity or perseverance). page × per_page must not exceed 10 000. Heavy searches (for example date, local-time or size filters without sol) may answer 503 service_busy with a Retry-After header; retry later or narrow the query.

Every v2 photo has two time fields. taken_at is Earth time (UTC). local_time ({"time": "17:40:12", "kind": "lmst", "planet": "mars"}) is the local solar time on Mars where the rover stood: lmst (mean time) for Curiosity, Perseverance and Ingenuity, ltst (true time) for Spirit and Opportunity. It is null when the source gives no time. With near, each item also has near_stop: the rover stop the photo is tied to (sol, site, drive, lat, lon) and distance_km to your point.

Photo lists (/api/v2/photos, /api/v2/rovers/{rover}/photos, .../photos/latest, /api/v2/photos/batch) accept fields, a comma-separated list of photo keys in lower case (for example fields=sol,img_src,camera). id and credit are always included. It only shrinks the response: it does not speed up searches or change rate limits. An unknown key gives 422. Each photo also has mast: the camera direction as azimuth (0 to 360) and elevation (-90 to 90) in degrees. The angles are relative to the rover body, not to north, so they are not a compass bearing. It is null when the source gives no angles.

GET /api/v2/rovers/curiosity/radiation returns one summary per sol from the RAD instrument (NASA PDS dataset MSL-M-RAD-3-RDR-V1.0), Curiosity only; other rovers answer 404 not_found. Optional sol_min and sol_max are inclusive; without them you get the whole history in one response. dose_b_ugy_day and dose_e_ugy_day (mean, min, max) are the absorbed dose rate in the instrument's detectors, in microgray per day. This is not equivalent dose: the data has no sieverts and we do not convert. The dataset lags the mission by months, so the latest sols are missing. The response always has credit and citation: keep them when you show or redistribute the data.

GET /api/v2/rovers/{rover}/weather returns one weather summary per sol for curiosity (REMS) and perseverance (MEDA), from NASA PDS; other rovers answer 404 not_found. Optional sol_min and sol_max are inclusive; no pagination. GET /api/v2/rovers/{rover}/weather/{sol} adds 24 hourly values (hours, local mean solar time) and the source files; a sol with no data gives 404 sol_not_found. Each hour has the mean (for example air_temp_c) and the lowest and highest valid values of the hour (air_temp_c_min, air_temp_c_max); the same for pressure, humidity, ground temperature and, for Curiosity, UV. An hour with no readings of a parameter has null in all three keys. Perseverance also has wind_speed_ms (hourly mean), wind_gust_ms (99th percentile of the hour's wind speed) and wind_direction_deg (most frequent direction); all three are null outside sols 36–313. The hourly keys are additions: clients that read only the means keep working. Sol Index shows them as charts at /rovers/{rover}/sols/{sol}/conditions.

Units: air and ground temperature in °C, pressure in Pa, relative humidity in %, UV in W/m², wind speed in m/s. Wind direction is an azimuth in degrees clockwise from north, as the source gives it; we do not say whether it is "from" or "to". min and max are the lowest and highest valid values of the sol; mean is the mean of the hourly means, so uneven sampling does not skew it, and it is always given. flags lists notes on a sol: partial_coverage (readings in fewer than 18 of 24 hours; the mean may lean to day or night), humidity_over_100 (values above 100 % are kept as they are), out_of_range (impossible values, ignored); skip codes you do not know. Humidity is marked preliminary in parameters: the instrument teams treat its calibration as preliminary.

Curiosity has no wind: the wind key is absent. Perseverance has wind only on sols 36–313 (wind: null elsewhere) and no UV; its ground temperature is an estimate (quality: "estimate" in parameters): brightness temperature from the MEDA TIRS upward infrared flux with emissivity 1, about ±3 K; sol 1 and incomplete sols are not computed (null). Curiosity's ground temperature is measured. null means the sensor exists but the sol has no valid value. The archive lags the mission by 6–8 months and is updated in batches, so the latest sols are missing (compare source_max_sol with max_sol of the rover). The response always has credit: keep it when you show or redistribute the data.

Interactive documentation of every endpoint: /docs. Machine-readable: /openapi.json, and a short reference for LLM assistants: /llms.txt.
